Marguerite runs the only laundromat in a town of four hundred people, and she has opinions about everyone's socks. She can tell who is in love by the fabric softener they choose, and who is lying by how they fold their towels. On Tuesday morning a stranger walks in wearing a long brown coat and carrying a single white shirt in a paper bag. He does not say hello. He puts the shirt in machine number seven, which everybody in town knows is haunted, and sits down to read a newspaper that is three weeks old.

Marguerite watches him for forty minutes. The machine spins, stops, spins again. Then it plays a tune, which it has never done before. "That machine doesn't do that," Marguerite says. The stranger turns a page. "It does for me," he says.

When the cycle ends, the shirt is gone and the bag contains a key. The stranger nods, puts the key in his pocket, and leaves without his shirt. Marguerite closes early for the first time in thirty years. That night she puts one of her own socks into machine number seven, just to see. In the morning there is a note in the drum. It says: Nice try.
